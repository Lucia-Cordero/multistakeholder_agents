## Assessment: TRANSEURO Trial Design and Patient Selection Adequacy (Clinical Perspective, as of Dec 2014)

**Bottom line:** TRANSEURO incorporates real lessons from prior failures, but three concerns temper confidence that the design is fully adequate to avoid repeating past problems.

---

### Concern 1: Absence of a sham-surgery control arm undermines efficacy interpretation
The two rigorous prior trials that used blinded, placebo-controlled designs (Freed et al., PMID 11236774; Olanow et al., PMID 12953276) both **failed their primary efficacy endpoints**, with benefit emerging only in post-hoc subgroups (younger patients; milder disease/four-donor arm). These trials taught the field that open-label uncontrolled grafting studies had substantially overestimated benefit relative to blinded comparisons — the original case-series/open-label reports (PMID 2786405, 1435880, 7700284) were far more optimistic than the controlled data. TRANSEURO, however, is explicitly an **open-label** study (PMID 25170676; PMID 23195419), reintroducing exactly the design vulnerability (unblinded assessment, surgical/placebo effect) that the double-blind trials were built to correct. Given how large placebo/expectation effects can be in PD surgical trials, an open-label design at this stage of an already twice-disappointing intervention is a significant methodological weakness for generating convincing efficacy evidence, even if it is defensible for a feasibility/safety-refinement study.

**Confidence: High** — this is a well-documented, structural design limitation directly traceable to the negative controlled-trial literature (PMID 11236774, 12953276, 20887880).

---

### Concern 2: Graft-induced dyskinesia (GID) mitigation strategies rest almost entirely on rodent-model inference, not clinical validation
GID was the dominant safety signal from prior trials (56% of transplant recipients in Olanow et al., PMID 12953276; 15% in Freed et al., PMID 11236774). The mechanistic hypotheses now guiding TRANSEURO's revised tissue preparation and placement — uneven/patchy reinnervation (PMID 16256359), graft size (PMID 16406222), serotonergic neuron contamination (PMID 22579773), and L-DOPA "priming" prior to grafting (PMID 19399877, 19393238) — are supported almost exclusively by **6-OHDA rat model data**, not by prospective human confirmation. The one clinical observation cited to support pharmacological rescue (buspirone reducing GID) comes from only three patients (referenced in PMID 22579773). Multiple contemporaneous reviews (PMID 20868333, 20887881, 21907087, 23195418, 23828589) emphasize that GID's underlying mechanism remains unresolved and that "significant differences in clinical trial protocols and lack of a truly representative animal model has hindered the search for a conclusive basis." Translating rodent-derived design fixes (tissue dissection protocols, graft placement, patient selection to reduce L-DOPA exposure/dyskinesia history) into a new human cohort carries meaningful residual uncertainty about whether GID will actually be prevented rather than merely reduced or delayed.

**Confidence: Medium-High** — the causal mechanisms are plausible and the design changes are rational, but they remain unproven in humans at the point TRANSEURO began grafting.

---

### Concern 3: Real-world patient selection shows enrollment bias that may not track the "optimal" profile derived from prior subgroup analyses
The controlled trials suggested benefit was concentrated in younger, milder patients (PMID 11236774, 12953276), and TRANSEURO's eligibility criteria appear designed around this. However, direct empirical data from the trial's own Cambridge cohort (PMID 25170676) show that patients actually recruited were younger, more educated, and cognitively higher-scoring than eligible non-enrolled patients — consistent with intended selection — but **also had significantly more motor complications (dyskinesias)** than the non-enrolled comparison group, and enrollment was strongly associated with being under the principal investigator's direct care (referral/access bias). Since pre-existing L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia has been mechanistically linked to greater susceptibility to GID after grafting in animal models (PMID 19399877, 19393238), enrolling patients with more baseline motor complications is somewhat in tension with the goal of minimizing GID risk. This also raises generalizability concerns: the enrolled population may not represent the broader PD population who might ultimately need this therapy, and access appears uneven (a single-center's referral practices driving selection).

**Confidence: Medium** — based on a single-center report (n=26 vs 33) that may not generalize to the full multi-center TRANSEURO cohort, but it is a direct empirical (not merely theoretical) finding from within the trial itself, which makes it a concrete rather than speculative concern.

---

### Additional lower-priority note
Long-term pathological findings of host-to-graft α-synuclein/Lewy body propagation 11–16 years post-transplant (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577) raise questions about durability of benefit and disease-modification claims, but this is a longer-horizon concern that does not immediately preclude proceeding with a safety/feasibility-oriented trial, and patients retained meaningful clinical benefit despite graft pathology (PMID 18391963, 24372386). This tempers rather than negates the overall justification.

---

**Overall clinical judgment:** The trial design shows genuine scientific learning from prior failures (patient stratification by age/severity, attention to tissue preparation and graft placement, awareness of GID mechanisms), providing partial justification to proceed. However, the lack of blinding/sham control, the unvalidated (rodent-derived) nature of the GID-mitigation strategy, and documented selection biases in actual enrollment collectively mean the trial design and patient selection are **only partially, not fully, adequate** given the specific safety and efficacy problems identified in the prior double-blind trials.
