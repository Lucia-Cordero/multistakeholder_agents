## Overall Assessment

From a strict biology/mechanism standpoint, the case for TRANSEURO's redesign **only partially** satisfies the stated success criterion. The literature by 2014 had converged on several candidate mechanisms for graft-induced dyskinesia (GID): (1) uneven/patchy dopaminergic reinnervation from small, spatially discrete graft deposits (PMID 16256359, 16406222); (2) contamination of the graft with serotonergic neurons that release dopamine in an unregulated, non-autoregulated fashion, evidenced by elevated striatal serotonin/dopamine transporter ratios in dyskinetic grafted patients and pharmacological reversal with 5-HT1A agonists (PMID 22579773, 21611977, 20887881); and (3) "priming" of the host by pre-existing chronic L-DOPA exposure/L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia before grafting (PMID 19399877, 19393238). The TRANSEURO program's public rationale (PMID 20489615, 21907087, 23237903, 24372386, 23401015) explicitly invokes better patient selection, tissue standardization, and placement/dosing refinements as lessons learned from the double-blind trials, but the evidence package does not demonstrate that the single most mechanistically direct and best-validated cause—serotonergic neuron contamination—is specifically targeted by a protocol change (e.g., dissection to exclude raphe/serotonergic precursors, or co-administration of 5-HT-modulating agents). This is a significant gap given the criterion as framed.

## Top Three Concerns

**1. Serotonergic contamination of the graft (high confidence this is inadequately addressed)**
The clinical PET/SPECT evidence directly linking GID to an elevated striatal serotonin/dopamine transporter ratio (PMID 21611977) and the pharmacological rat model showing that dopaminergic grafts—but only in combination with serotonergic hyperinnervation susceptibility—produce dyskinesia reversible by 5-HT1A/1B agonists (PMID 22579773) constitute the most mechanistically specific and best-corroborated (human + rodent) causal account of GID available by end-2014. None of the TRANSEURO description sources in the package (PMID 20489615, 21907087, 23237903, 24372386) specify a dissection or tissue-processing change designed to reduce serotonergic neuron content in the graft, nor an adjunct pharmacological strategy targeting 5-HT tone. Given that this is arguably the leading mechanistic hypothesis, its absence from the stated redesign rationale is a material weakness. **Confidence: high** that this constitutes an inadequately addressed mechanistic gap.

**2. Graft placement/size (uneven reinnervation) — partially but not fully addressed (moderate confidence)**
Rat data show that dyskinesia severity correlates with patchy, uneven dopaminergic reinnervation from small, single-site deposits (PMID 16256359) and that larger grafts and specific placement (caudal vs rostral putamen) modulate dyskinesia expression (16406222, 20887880). TRANSEURO's general description references "graft placement" and dosing lessons as part of its redesign (PMID 20489615, 21907087, 23401015), suggesting some mechanistic uptake, but the evidence package does not provide granular protocol detail (e.g., number of deposits, donor tissue quantity, exact target coordinates) sufficient to confirm this has been rigorously translated into a validated surgical solution. **Confidence: moderate** — directionally addressed but not demonstrably solved with same rigor as needed.

**3. L-DOPA priming / patient selection may work against the mechanistic lesson (moderate-high confidence)**
Preclinical data indicate that pre-graft chronic L-DOPA exposure (and pre-existing L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia) increases the risk and severity of subsequent GID/amphetamine-induced AIMs (PMID 19399877, 19393238). Yet the one TRANSEURO-specific empirical source in the package describing actual patient selection (PMID 25170676) reports that enrolled patients had "more motor complications" (i.e., more L-DOPA-associated dyskinesia) than eligible non-enrolled patients, alongside better baseline motor function and cognition. This suggests that real-world enrollment practices may be selecting exactly the "primed" phenotype mechanistic studies flag as high-risk for GID, rather than excluding it. This is a concrete tension between the stated mechanistic rationale (avoid GID) and observed trial-level patient selection behavior. **Confidence: moderate-high** that this represents a real inconsistency, tempered by the fact that this is only single-center enrollment data (Cambridge cohort) and may not reflect the full multi-center protocol's formal inclusion/exclusion criteria.

## Bottom Line
The redesign shows clear awareness of the GID literature and incorporates plausible mitigations for graft placement/size and patient selection broadly, but the evidence package does not show that the strongest mechanistic candidate—serotonergic neuron contamination/5-HT-dopamine imbalance—is explicitly and specifically countered in the tissue preparation or surgical protocol, and there are signals that real-world patient selection may not fully exclude the L-DOPA-priming risk factor. On the specific success criterion (adequate mechanistic address of the known cause of GID), the justification is **incomplete**, not absent — a partial rather than full pass.
