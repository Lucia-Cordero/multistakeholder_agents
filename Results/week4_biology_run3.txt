## Assessment: Does the TRANSEURO redesign adequately address the known cause of graft-induced dyskinesia (GID)?

**Bottom line:** The premise of the assessment question is itself the central biological problem. By 2014, GID does not have a single, mechanistically validated "known cause" — it has several partially-supported, partially-conflicting candidate mechanisms. TRANSEURO's redesign addresses some of these candidates (patient selection, standardized placement) but the evidence package does not demonstrate it directly neutralizes the mechanism with the strongest human mechanistic support (serotonergic hyperinnervation). This is a meaningful gap in mechanistic justification, though not necessarily fatal to proceeding cautiously.

### Concern 1 — No convergent, validated single cause of GID exists to "address"
The literature by 2014 presents at least four non-mutually-exclusive candidate mechanisms: (a) uneven/patchy dopaminergic reinnervation depending on graft placement (PMID 16256359), (b) graft size/dose effects (PMID 16406222), (c) pre-graft chronic L-DOPA "priming" (PMID 19399877, 19393238), and (d) serotonergic neuron contamination of the graft causing dysregulated false-transmitter dopamine release (PMID 21611977, 22579773). Multiple contemporaneous reviews explicitly state the mechanism remains unresolved and multifactorial (PMID 20868333, 20887881, 21907087, 23195418, 23828589). A redesign cannot be said to "adequately address the known cause" when no single known cause has been established — at best it can hedge against several plausible contributors.
**Confidence: High.** This is directly and repeatedly stated across independent review sources spanning 2010–2013.

### Concern 2 — The strongest human mechanistic evidence (serotonergic hyperinnervation) is not clearly matched by a corresponding fix in the redesign, and is itself contradicted by preclinical data
The most compelling *causal* (not just correlative) human evidence for GID is the demonstration that a patient with long-standing GID had an elevated striatal serotonin/dopamine transporter ratio, and that pharmacological suppression of serotonergic neuron activity (buspirone, 5-HT1A/1B agonists) abolished the dyskinesia (PMID 21611977, 22579773). This points to serotonergic contamination of the donor ventral mesencephalic dissection — tissue that inevitably includes adjacent raphe-derived serotonergic neurons — as a key proximal driver. However, a rat model study explicitly designed to test this found that the dopamine-to-serotonin cell ratio and innervation density did *not* significantly correlate with GID severity once sufficient dopamine neurons were present, suggesting the serotonergic contribution may be minor in that model (PMID 22759908). This translational discordance means confidence in serotonergic contamination as "the" cause is only moderate, and — more importantly for TRANSEURO — the evidence package contains no explicit demonstration that the redesigned dissection/preparation protocol was altered to minimize serotonergic neuron content, which would be the direct biological corollary of the leading human-derived mechanistic hypothesis.
**Confidence: Moderate-high** that this is a real gap; **moderate** that it would materially change outcomes given the conflicting preclinical data.

### Concern 3 — TRANSEURO's actual stated design changes target correlates (patient selection, graft placement, standardization) rather than the graft's biological composition
Available descriptions of the TRANSEURO rationale (PMID 20489615, 23237903, 24372386, 25170676) emphasize patient selection (younger, earlier-stage, presumably shorter L-DOPA exposure/priming history), standardized surgical/placement technique, and tissue quality control — plausible responses to the graft-placement/uneven-reinnervation hypothesis (PMID 16256359) and the L-DOPA priming hypothesis (PMID 19399877). These are reasonable, evidence-informed mitigations for *some* candidate mechanisms. But because the etiology is multifactorial and not fully reconciled (Concern 1), and because the serotonergic/dopaminergic ratio mechanism with the clearest human causal signature is not explicitly addressed by compositional changes to the graft tissue itself (Concern 2), the redesign's mechanistic coverage is partial rather than comprehensive.
**Confidence: Moderate.** Based on inference from available program descriptions rather than a detailed dissection protocol document, which was not retrievable in this evidence set.

### Overall judgment (Biology lens only)
The redesign is scientifically reasonable and incorporates lessons from the two sham-controlled trials (PMID 11236774, 12953276) regarding patient selection and graft placement, but it does not demonstrate that it has resolved or even fully engaged with the leading human-derived mechanistic hypothesis for chronic off-medication GID (serotonergic hyperinnervation, PMID 21611977). Given that GID's mechanism remains contested and only partially reconciled between clinical PET data and rodent models, proceeding to grafting is justifiable as an incremental, hypothesis-testing step — but the claim that the mechanistic rationale "adequately addresses the known cause" of GID is **not well supported**, because no single known cause has been established, and the redesign's addressed risk factors do not clearly map onto the strongest candidate mechanism.
