# CLINICAL Assessment: TRANSEURO Grafting Decision (as of Dec 31, 2014)

## Overall framing

The evidence package documents a roughly 25-year arc: encouraging but modest open-label results (1989–1995), followed by two NIH-funded double-blind, sham-controlled trials that failed to show robust efficacy and revealed a serious, previously unrecognized adverse effect—graft-induced dyskinesia (GID)—followed by roughly a decade of largely preclinical (rodent) mechanistic work aimed at explaining GID, and finally a set of review articles arguing the field is ready to try again. TRANSEURO is justified by proponents as incorporating "lessons learned," but the package contains **little direct evidence that the specific mitigations proposed have been validated in humans**, and contains at least one piece of trial-specific evidence (PMID 25170676) raising new selection concerns. On balance, the clinical case for proceeding is plausible but not strongly de-risked by the available evidence.

## Top three concerns

### 1. GID mitigation strategies are preclinical/rodent-derived and unvalidated in humans — HIGH CONFIDENCE this is a material gap

The two controlled human trials that established the GID problem reported alarming rates: 56% of transplanted patients developed off-medication dyskinesia in Olanow's trial (PMID 12953276), and 15% developed persistent dystonia/dyskinesia in Freed's trial even after medication withdrawal (PMID 11236774). The subsequent decade of research proposed multiple candidate mechanisms—uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation and graft placement (PMID 16256359), graft size (PMID 16406222), pre-graft chronic levodopa "priming" (PMID 19399877, PMID 19393238), and serotonergic contamination of the graft (PMID 22579773)—but **every one of these mechanistic studies is a rodent 6-OHDA model study**, not a human trial. The only human data cited on a candidate treatment (buspirone reducing GID) is described secondhand, in three patients, within a rodent-focused paper (PMID 22579773), not as a validated clinical intervention. No source in this package reports a completed human trial demonstrating that a redesigned protocol actually lowers GID incidence below the 15–56% range observed in the original controlled trials. Proceeding to grafting patients on the strength of rat-model mechanistic insight, without an intervening human safety trial confirming reduced GID rates, is a genuine gap between "we understand the mechanism better" (asserted in PMID 23401015, 20887880, 21907087) and "we have shown this can be prevented in patients."

### 2. Efficacy signal from the only rigorous (blinded, sham-controlled) trials was weak/subgroup-limited, and TRANSEURO appears to reintroduce an open-label design — MODERATE-HIGH CONFIDENCE

The original open-label case series (PMID 2786405, 1435880, 7700284) generated optimism, but PMID 2786405 itself explicitly cautioned in 1989 that "widespread clinical trials... are probably not warranted at this time." When rigorously tested with blinding and sham surgery, benefit was inconsistent: Freed found significant improvement only in patients ≤60 years old, not overall (PMID 11236774); Olanow found no significant overall treatment effect, with a signal only in a milder-disease subgroup, concluding transplantation "currently cannot be recommended as a therapy for PD" (PMID 12953276). This pattern—positive open-label results not replicating under blinding—is a classic placebo-confound signature. PMID 25170676 explicitly describes TRANSEURO as an "open label multi-centre surgical transplant trial," which reintroduces exactly the design vulnerability (unblinded assessment, placebo/expectation effects) that the earlier controlled trials were built to eliminate. The package does not contain evidence of a sham-surgery arm or blinded primary outcome for TRANSEURO, which is a step backward in rigor relative to the trials that most credibly established both the efficacy ceiling and the GID risk.

### 3. Patient selection/enrollment appears to enrich for exactly the risk factors associated with poor outcomes and GID — MODERATE CONFIDENCE

PMID 25170676, a source specifically about TRANSEURO enrollment, found that enrolled patients were younger and more cognitively/educationally advantaged than eligible non-enrolled patients (consistent with the Freed finding that younger patients benefit more, PMID 11236774)—a reasonable selection choice. However, the same source found enrolled patients had **more motor complications** (i.e., more baseline dyskinesia/fluctuations) than non-enrolled eligible patients. This is concerning in light of the levodopa-priming literature: PMID 19399877 and PMID 19393238 both show in rodent models that pre-graft chronic levodopa exposure and L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia priming increase susceptibility to graft-related abnormal involuntary movements and reduce graft efficacy. A trial that selectively enrolls patients with more motor complications (a proxy for more L-dopa exposure/dyskinesia history) may be selecting a population mechanistically primed for higher GID risk, even while otherwise selecting for favorable age/cognition. Additionally, this evidence comes from a single center's cohort (n=26 enrolled, n=33 comparison), limiting generalizability of the finding itself—hence moderate rather than high confidence.

## Secondary concern noted but weighted lower

Long-term propagation of Lewy pathology into grafted neurons (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577, 21901584) raises questions about durability of any benefit and the disease's capacity to "infect" healthy grafted tissue over 11–16 years. This is a legitimate long-horizon concern but does not bear directly on the immediate safety/efficacy adequacy of trial design and patient selection for initial grafting, so I rank it below the three above.

## Bottom line

Given the CLINICAL success criterion (adequacy of trial design and patient selection relative to prior safety/efficacy findings), the evidence package supports a **cautious, not clearly favorable** verdict: the scientific rationale for refinement is reasonable and well-articulated in review literature (PMID 23401015, 20887880, 24372386), but the package lacks direct human evidence that the proposed GID mitigations work, documents a design choice (open-label) that reintroduces a known confound, and contains a specific enrollment-pattern finding (PMID 25170676) suggesting the actual recruited population may carry elevated GID risk factors. Proceeding is defensible as a research step but is not strongly justified as a de-risked clinical advance based on this evidence alone.
