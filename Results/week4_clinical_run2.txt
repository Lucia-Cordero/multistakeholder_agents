## Assessment: TRANSEURO Trial Design and Patient Selection (Clinical Perspective)

**Overall judgment:** Partially justified but with significant unresolved concerns. TRANSEURO incorporates real lessons from the two negative sham-controlled trials, but three issues suggest the design/selection may not adequately protect patients or ensure a clean answer to the efficacy question.

---

### Concern 1: Patient selection criteria may not translate into an unbiased or representative trial population, and may preferentially enroll patients at elevated dyskinesia risk
**Confidence: High**

The retrospective analysis of the Cambridge TRANSEURO cohort (PMID 25170676) directly shows that enrolled patients differed systematically from eligible-but-unenrolled patients: younger, more educated, higher cognitive scores, and—critically—**better motor function but *more* motor complications** than non-enrolled eligible patients. This is concerning because motor complications (dyskinesias) at baseline could interact with graft biology. Preclinical work strongly indicates that pre-graft L-DOPA "priming" and pre-existing L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia increase the risk and severity of graft-induced dyskinesia (GID) after transplantation (PMID 19399877, 19393238). If the actual enrolled population is enriched for patients with more baseline motor complications (a marker of dyskinesia-prone physiology), the trial may be selecting for exactly the group most vulnerable to the therapy's most serious documented adverse event, even while nominally targeting "younger, milder" patients based on the age-stratified benefit seen in Freed et al. (PMID 11236774). This is a real-world selection-bias problem not fully addressed by the trial's formal inclusion criteria.

### Concern 2: Graft-induced dyskinesia mechanism remains incompletely resolved, and mitigation strategies in TRANSEURO rest on multiple partially-validated hypotheses rather than a single confirmed causal pathway
**Confidence: Moderate-High**

GID was the major safety signal from both sham-controlled trials (56% incidence of off-medication dyskinesia in Olanow et al., PMID 12953276; dystonia/dyskinesia recurrence in 15% of Freed et al. patients, PMID 11236774). The mechanistic literature offers several candidate causes: uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation from small, poorly-placed grafts (PMID 16256359), graft size (PMID 16406222), serotonergic neuron contamination of VM tissue causing dysregulated dopamine release (PMID 22579773), and pre-graft L-DOPA priming (PMID 19399877, 19393238). Reviews as late as 2010-2013 (PMID 20868333, 20887880, 21907087, 23195418, 23828589) explicitly state that no single mechanism has been confirmed and that a "truly representative animal model" is still lacking. TRANSEURO's design (larger, more standardized grafts placed to achieve more even reinnervation, tighter tissue-quality control) addresses several of these hypotheses simultaneously, which is reasonable risk-mitigation, but the trial is effectively testing multiple unproven assumptions at once in patients, rather than validating the fix for a single well-characterized cause before wider grafting. Given GID's irreversibility and disability, this multi-hypothesis approach in a first-in-cohort re-launch is a meaningful residual risk.

### Concern 3: Long-term biological uncertainty—host-to-graft propagation of synucleinopathy—raises questions about durability and adequacy of long-term outcome/monitoring design
**Confidence: Moderate**

Autopsy data show Lewy body pathology developing in grafted neurons 11-16 years post-transplantation (PMID 18391963), with mechanistic support for prion-like α-synuclein transfer from host to graft (PMID 20846907, 21245577, 21626551, 22936307). While the same source data suggest most grafted neurons remain functionally intact for a decade and patients can retain benefit, this raises a fundamental unresolved question: does the underlying PD disease process eventually compromise graft-derived benefit, and if so, over what time horizon? This isn't primarily a "trial design flaw" in the conventional sense, but it means TRANSEURO's outcome assessment window and consent/expectation-setting for patients should explicitly account for the possibility of delayed graft dysfunction—a consideration that is acknowledged in the literature (PMID 24372386) but does not appear to translate into a specific long-term monitoring or biomarker strategy distinct from prior trials.

---

### Secondary observation
The two prior double-blind trials found treatment effects only in *post hoc* subgroups (age ≤60 in Freed et al., PMID 11236774; milder disease severity in Olanow et al., PMID 12953276)—different stratifying variables in each trial. TRANSEURO's selection criteria draw on both lessons, but the fact that the "responder" subgroup identification differed between the two prior trials suggests the true predictive patient-selection variable(s) for efficacy are still not firmly established, adding uncertainty to whether TRANSEURO's chosen inclusion criteria will successfully enrich for responders.

**Bottom line:** The trial design reflects substantial and thoughtful learning from prior failures (mechanistic GID research, age/severity stratification signals, tissue/surgical standardization), which is a genuine strength. However, documented selection-bias in actual recruitment (PMID 25170676), incomplete mechanistic closure on GID risk, and unresolved long-term disease-propagation biology mean the assessment of "adequately justified" is conditional—reasonable to proceed under careful monitoring, but not without meaningful residual clinical risk that prior trials' findings have not fully resolved.
