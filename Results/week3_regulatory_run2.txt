## REGULATORY ASSESSMENT: TRANSEURO Grafting Decision (as of Dec 31, 2014)

**Overall judgment: Conditionally justified to proceed, but risk characterization is incomplete in ways that matter for full regulatory/ethical sign-off.** The known adverse-event profile is unusually well characterized for an experimental neurosurgical therapy (25+ years of open-label and controlled trial data), and the sponsor has explicitly built protocol modifications around lessons learned. However, several risks remain only partially mitigated, and the mitigation strategy itself is largely untested prospectively in humans at the point grafting is set to begin.

---

### Top Concern 1: Graft-Induced Dyskinesia (GID) — well-characterized mechanistically, but mitigation is unproven in a live protocol
**Confidence: Moderate**

GID is the single best-documented serious adverse event of this intervention: 56% incidence with off-medication dyskinesia in the Olanow double-blind trial (PMID 12953276), also reported in Freed et al. (PMID 11236774). A substantial preclinical/mechanistic literature (PMID 16256359, 16406222, 19399877, 19393238, 22579773, 20868333, 20887881, 21907087, 23195418, 23828589) converges on plausible causes — uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation, graft size, pre-graft L-DOPA priming, serotonergic:dopaminergic neuron ratio in the graft. TRANSEURO's design (multiple deposits, refined dissection protocol, patient selection criteria) is explicitly informed by this body of work (PMID 20489615, 21901584, 23237903, 24372386, 23401015).

This is a case of good hazard *identification* but unproven hazard *mitigation*: no completed human trial as of Dec 2014 has prospectively demonstrated that the proposed changes (placement strategy, tissue standardization, patient selection) actually reduce GID incidence below the ~15–56% rates seen in earlier trials. Regulatory sign-off is therefore resting substantially on rat-model extrapolation (e.g., PMID 16256359, 19399877) rather than confirmed clinical mitigation — an acceptable but non-trivial residual uncertainty for a serious, potentially irreversible motor side effect in a treatment-refractory population.

### Top Concern 2: Host-to-graft disease propagation (Lewy pathology/α-synuclein transfer) — characterized descriptively, not mitigated, and long-term implications unresolved
**Confidence: Low (confidence that this is adequately addressed)**

Multiple autopsy/mechanistic studies show that grafted fetal neurons can develop Lewy body pathology 11–16 years post-transplant, implying host-to-graft "prion-like" α-synuclein propagation (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577, 21626551, 22936307, 24372386). This is a genuinely novel risk category not present in most other cell-therapy contexts, and it was discovered only through long-term follow-up of prior trial cohorts — meaning any newly grafted TRANSEURO patient is enrolling in an intervention whose durability ceiling and long-term disease-modifying character is still unknown at consent. No mitigation exists in the current protocol (no anti-synuclein aggregation strategy is incorporated into TRANSEURO), and the "reassurance" cited (most grafted neurons remain functional after a decade — PMID 18391963, 24372386) is based on very small numbers of long-surviving patients. From a regulatory standpoint, this risk is disclosed and monitorable but not mitigated — it is being managed by informed consent and imaging/monitoring rather than by any protocol design change. This is defensible as "adequately characterized for consent purposes" but should not be conflated with "adequately mitigated."

### Top Concern 3: Uncertain efficacy signal and non-representative trial population undermine the risk-benefit justification and equitable-access standard
**Confidence: Moderate**

Both prior sham-surgery-controlled trials—the only Class I evidence available—failed their primary endpoints (PMID 11236774: no significant global rating change; PMID 12953276: p=0.244 overall), with benefit confined to post-hoc subgroups (younger patients, milder disease). This means the population-level efficacy that would justify accepting known risks (GID, surgical risk, immunosuppression, fetal tissue sourcing burden) is still not established by controlled data; TRANSEURO is essentially re-testing a refined hypothesis in a population selected on the basis of subgroup findings, which is scientifically reasonable but means the underlying risk/benefit ratio for the *specific* protocol has not itself been validated prospectively.

Compounding this, PMID 25170676 (the TRANSEURO Cambridge cohort study) directly shows that enrolled patients are younger, more educated, cognitively higher-functioning, and disproportionately drawn from the PI's own clinic compared to the broader eligible PD population. This raises a specific regulatory/ethical concern about equipoise, generalizability of any positive result, and equitable trial access — relevant to whether the informed consent and patient-selection process meets ethical trial-conduct standards, independent of biological safety.

Additional lower-order concerns feeding into this judgment: fetal tissue sourcing remains ethically and logistically constrained (multiple donors per patient required historically, e.g. PMID 1435880, 11236774; NECTAR ethical guidelines PMID 7897446 provide some European regulatory precedent but predate current protocol); immunosuppression regimens (cyclosporine/azathioprine/steroids, PMID 2786405, 1435880) carry standard but non-trivial infection/toxicity risk that is well-precedented and manageable.

### Summary Table

| Concern | Characterized? | Mitigated? | Confidence in adequacy for approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graft-induced dyskinesia | Yes — extensively | Proposed but clinically unproven | Moderate |
| Host-to-graft synucleinopathy | Yes — descriptively | No specific mitigation; monitoring only | Low |
| Efficacy uncertainty / trial population representativeness | Yes | Partial (subgroup-informed design); access equity unresolved | Moderate |

**Bottom line for REGULATORY:** Proceeding is defensible given three decades of cumulative safety/mechanistic data and explicit protocol refinements, but full ethical/regulatory confidence would be strengthened by (1) an explicit interim safety stopping rule keyed to GID incidence, (2) enhanced/standardized informed consent language addressing the unresolved long-term synucleinopathy risk and its uncertain implications, and (3) documented efforts to broaden recruitment representativeness. As designed and evidenced through Dec 2014, this is an **approvable but not fully de-risked** proceed decision.
