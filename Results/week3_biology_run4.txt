## BIOLOGY ASSESSMENT — TRANSEURO Grafting Decision (as of Dec 31, 2014)

**Bottom line: NOT adequately justified on mechanistic grounds.** The redesign incorporates several sensible, evidence-informed modifications, but as of this date there is no single "known cause" of graft-induced dyskinesia (GID) to address — the field had converged on a set of *competing, only partially tested* hypotheses, and TRANSEURO's protocol changes plausibly mitigate some but not others, with no direct preclinical or clinical confirmation that the combined redesign prevents GID.

### Top Three Concerns

**1. GID etiology remains multifactorial and unresolved, not a single "known cause" — HIGH CONFIDENCE**
The evidence base explicitly frames GID as an open mechanistic question through 2014, not a solved problem. Review after review lists parallel, non-exclusive hypotheses: uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation from small deposit grafts (PMID 16256359), graft size/dose effects (PMID 16406222), pregraft L-DOPA "priming" (PMID 19399877, 19393238), serotonergic neuron contamination and 5-HT/DA receptor imbalance (PMID 22579773), and host neuroinflammatory/gliotic responses (PMID 8797670). Synthesis papers as late as 2011–2013 state plainly that "questions still remain as to the underlying mechanism" (PMID 21907087) and treat these as competing rather than converging hypotheses (PMID 20868333, 23195418, 23828589). Even the more optimistic 2013 Lindvall commentary (PMID 23401015) asserts mechanisms are "much better understood" without citing a definitive causal test. A redesign cannot be said to "adequately address the known cause" when no consensus single cause exists — at best it can be said to hedge against several candidate risk factors simultaneously, which is a weaker justification than the language of the trial rationale implies.

**2. Serotonergic contamination hypothesis — the most mechanistically specific candidate — appears inadequately operationalized in the redesign — MODERATE-HIGH CONFIDENCE**
The most mechanistically concrete and pharmacologically validated hypothesis in the package is that dysregulated, non-autoregulated dopamine release from co-transplanted serotonergic neurons drives GID (PMID 22579773 — buspirone/5-HT1A,1B agonism suppresses amphetamine-induced GID in rats; consistent with clinical PET/buspirone data referenced therein). This is a specific, testable, biologically plausible mechanism (5-HT terminals lack DA autoreceptor feedback, causing "false transmitter" release and swings in extracellular DA). However, nothing in the evidence package (including the TRANSEURO-specific sources, PMID 20489615, 24372386, 25170676) describes tissue dissection or purification protocols specifically designed to minimize serotonergic neuron content in the graft. Ventral mesencephalon dissection inherently risks including raphe-adjacent tissue, and no quality-control metric (e.g., 5-HT:DA neuron ratio) is reported as part of the redesign. If serotonergic contamination is indeed a/the major driver, TRANSEURO's improvements (standardized single-donor-per-site protocols, refined targeting) do not clearly address this specific biological mechanism.

**3. Patient-selection and priming-related mitigations are only partially supported and may not eliminate GID risk — MODERATE CONFIDENCE**
TRANSEURO recruits younger, earlier-stage, less L-DOPA-exposed patients (PMID 25170676), which aligns with the hypothesis that chronic pregraft L-DOPA exposure ("priming") sensitizes the striatum to GID (PMID 19399877, 19393238). This is a reasonable mechanistically-motivated design choice. However, the same preclinical literature is explicit that "GID-like behaviors occur regardless of pregraft levodopa status" (PMID 19399877) — meaning priming may modulate severity but does not by itself account for GID occurrence, and its elimination does not guarantee prevention. Additionally, addressing graft placement/reinnervation evenness (via multiple deposits, informed by PMID 16256359) is a targeted, mechanistically coherent improvement for *one* candidate pathway, but the evidence package does not demonstrate that this single change (nor combined with patient selection) is sufficient given the multifactorial nature of the problem (concern #1). Confidence is moderate because the rat data support the biological plausibility of these mitigations individually, but no combined preclinical validation of the full TRANSEURO protocol against GID is present in the evidence base.

### Summary Judgment
From a pure biology/mechanism standpoint, TRANSEURO's redesign reflects genuine, evidence-based engineering against several plausible contributing factors to GID (graft placement/reinnervation pattern, patient/disease-stage selection, standardized tissue handling). However, the claim that it "addresses the known cause" overstates the state of mechanistic knowledge in 2014: GID causation was still an active, contested research question with at least four non-mutually-exclusive candidate mechanisms, the most pharmacologically compelling of which (serotonergic contamination) does not appear to be directly targeted by the stated redesign features in this evidence package. Proceeding to grafting is defensible as a hypothesis-testing clinical experiment but is **not yet mechanistically de-risked** to the degree implied by a redesign claiming to solve the GID problem.
