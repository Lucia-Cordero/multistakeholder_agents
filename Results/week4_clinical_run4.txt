## Assessment: TRANSEURO Trial Design and Patient Selection (Clinical Perspective)

Based on the evidence package, TRANSEURO represents a scientifically motivated attempt to address well-documented failures of prior fetal VM transplantation trials, but several design and patient-selection issues remain incompletely resolved. My top three concerns, in order of severity:

### 1. Loss of blinded/placebo control undermines ability to demonstrate genuine efficacy — **HIGH CONFIDENCE**

The two rigorous prior trials that used double-blind, sham-surgery designs (Freed 2001, PMID 11236774; Olanow 2003, PMID 12953276) were essential precisely because open-label experience in the 1990s (PMID 2786405, 1435880) had generated enthusiasm that did not survive blinded testing — overall primary endpoints were negative in both controlled trials, with benefit confined to post-hoc subgroups (younger patients, milder disease). TRANSEURO is explicitly designed as an **open-label** trial (PMID 20489615, 24372386). Given that placebo/expectation effects in PD surgical trials are large and well-documented, and given that the field's most informative negative data came specifically from unblinding the sham effect, reverting to an open-label design makes it difficult to distinguish true graft-derived motor benefit from surgical placebo response. This is a substantial step back methodologically from the trial designs that generated the most trustworthy prior data, even though there are practical/ethical reasons (tissue scarcity, ethical concerns about repeated sham burr-holes) for the change. The rationale documents (PMID 20489615, 24372386) do not fully resolve how efficacy will be attributed without a concurrent control arm.

### 2. GID mitigation strategy rests substantially on rodent-model mechanistic data with limited human validation — **MODERATE CONFIDENCE**

A large body of preclinical work has identified plausible contributors to graft-induced dyskinesia: uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation and graft placement (PMID 16256359), graft size (PMID 16406222), pre-graft chronic L-DOPA "priming" (PMID 19399877, 19393238), and serotonergic neuron contamination of the graft (PMID 22579773, 20868333, 20887881, 21907087, 23195418, 23828589). These mechanistic insights plausibly inform TRANSEURO's refinements (standardized dissection, graft placement protocols, tissue composition control). However, most of the causal mechanistic work is from 6-OHDA rat models using amphetamine-induced AIMs as a proxy for a phenomenon that in humans occurs spontaneously off-medication — a translational gap acknowledged by multiple reviews in the package (PMID 20868333, 20887881, 23195418). Critically, essentially all human PD candidates present with a history of years of chronic L-DOPA use, so the "pre-graft L-DOPA priming" risk factor identified preclinically may be largely unavoidable in the real patient population, and the trial's ability to actually eliminate GID risk (rather than simply hope to reduce it) is unproven prospectively in humans as of this evidence base.

### 3. Documented selection bias in actual TRANSEURO enrollment raises generalizability and safety-interaction concerns — **HIGH CONFIDENCE**

Direct empirical data on TRANSEURO's own recruitment (PMID 25170676, Cambridge cohort) show that enrolled patients were significantly younger, more educated, and had higher cognitive scores than PD patients eligible for but not enrolled in the trial, and that enrollment was also associated with being under the care of the site PI (a referral/access bias). While selecting younger patients aligns with the identified predictor of benefit in the Freed trial (PMID 11236774), the same study found enrolled patients had **more motor complications** (i.e., more baseline dyskinesia) than non-enrolled eligible patients — this is concerning because chronic pre-graft L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia/priming has been mechanistically linked to greater risk and severity of graft-induced dyskinesia in animal models (PMID 19399877, 19393238). This suggests the actual enrolled population may not optimally reflect the "ideal" low-GID-risk profile the trial's rationale intends to select for, and raises questions about equitable access and how representative efficacy findings will be for the broader PD population.

---

**Additional lower-priority observations:** The finding of host-to-graft α-synuclein/Lewy body propagation (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577, 21626551) raises long-term disease-modification concerns but is not disqualifying for proceeding, since multiple sources note grafts remain functionally beneficial for over a decade despite this pathology (PMID 18391963, 24372386) — this is more a long-term mechanistic caveat than an immediate trial-design flaw.

**Overall judgment:** The program incorporates substantial, hard-won lessons from prior trials (tissue standardization, patient age/severity stratification, mechanistic GID research) and is not proceeding blindly — but the shift to an open-label design against a backdrop of two prior *negative* blinded trials, incomplete human validation of GID-avoidance strategies, and documented selection bias in actual recruitment together constitute real, non-trivial adequacy concerns that should temper confidence in cleanly interpretable trial results, even if proceeding is defensible on ethical/scientific-knowledge-generation grounds.
