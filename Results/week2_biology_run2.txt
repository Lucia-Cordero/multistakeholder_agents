## Overview

The specific success criterion is narrow: **does the mechanistic rationale for TRANSEURO's redesign adequately address the *known* cause of graft-induced dyskinesia (GID)?** Based solely on this evidence package, my assessment is that it does **not**, primarily because the package itself shows that as of December 2014 there was no single "known cause" of GID — there were several partially competing hypotheses, tested mostly in an imperfect animal model, with at least one internal tension in the data that the trial rationale does not resolve.

## Top three concerns

**1. The cause of GID was not actually established — it remained multifactorial and contested. (High confidence)**

Multiple review sources in the package explicitly say the mechanism is unresolved. PMID 20868333 lists at least four unreconciled hypotheses: graft composition, graft size, L-DOPA exposure/priming, and inflammatory response. PMID 21907087 states directly that "significant differences in clinical trial protocols and lack of a truly representative animal model has hindered the search for a conclusive basis for their appearance." PMID 22579773 begins by stating "the mechanism underlying the appearance of GID is unknown," even while reporting a serotonergic/D2-receptor modulatory role. Separately, PMID 16256359 implicates uneven/patchy reinnervation from graft placement, while PMID 19399877 and PMID 19393238 implicate pre-graft L-DOPA priming and its neuroplastic sequelae. These are not the same mechanism, and the package does not contain evidence that they were reconciled into a single validated causal pathway before TRANSEURO proceeded. A trial rationale that addresses one plausible contributor (e.g., patient selection or graft placement) cannot be said to address "the known cause" when the evidence base itself presents the cause as plural and unsettled.

**2. The main mitigation is validated only in a preclinical model whose translational fidelity to human GID is explicitly questioned. (Moderate-high confidence)**

Much of the mechanistic work behind the redesign (graft placement, PMID 16256359; graft size, PMID 16406222; L-DOPA priming, PMID 19399877/19393238; serotonergic contribution, PMID 22579773) relies on the amphetamine-induced abnormal involuntary movements (AIMs) model in 6-OHDA-lesioned rats. But PMID 20868333, PMID 21907087, and PMID 23195418 all note that this model is an imperfect proxy — the human phenomenon is *spontaneous*, off-medication dyskinesia, whereas the rodent model requires pharmacological provocation (amphetamine) and does not fully replicate the clinical syndrome. This means that any redesign feature justified by "it reduced AIMs in the rat model" carries meaningfully weaker evidentiary support for preventing GID in human recipients than the trial rationale implies.

**3. There is an unresolved internal contradiction between the two leading structural hypotheses (patchy reinnervation vs. graft size) that the redesign does not visibly reconcile. (Moderate confidence)**

PMID 16256359 suggests GID arises from *uneven, patchy* striatal reinnervation from small, discrete graft deposits — implying that larger or more distributed grafts (more donors, better coverage) should reduce risk. But PMID 16406222, using the same general experimental paradigm, found that *larger* grafts produced *more severe* amphetamine-induced AIMs than smaller ones. If TRANSEURO's redesign (per the general descriptions in PMID 20489615, 23237903, 24372386) increases graft completeness/coverage to solve the patchiness problem, the package contains direct rodent evidence that this could exacerbate, not mitigate, the size-related component of GID risk. None of the trial-rationale sources in this package explicitly engage with or resolve this tension.

## Secondary observations (lower confidence, worth flagging)

- The package does not include a detailed TRANSEURO tissue-dissection or surgical protocol paper. The serotonergic-contamination hypothesis (PMID 22579773, building on buspirone response referenced from outside this package) is one of the more mechanistically specific leads, but I cannot confirm from the sources here whether TRANSEURO's tissue preparation specifically targets exclusion of serotonergic (raphe) contamination. This is a gap in the *evidence provided*, not necessarily in the trial design itself, and I flag it rather than assume either way.
- PMID 25170676 shows the actual TRANSEURO-enrolled cohort had "more motor complications" than eligible non-enrollees. Given PMID 19399877's finding that pre-existing L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia/priming affects post-graft outcomes, this raises a question about whether patient-selection criteria (age, PMID 11236774) adequately offset a possibly elevated baseline priming-related risk — the package does not resolve this.
- The Lewy-body/host-to-graft propagation findings (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577, 22936307) are a distinct biological concern (long-term graft integrity/disease process), not directly about GID mechanism, so I have not counted them among the top three, but they underscore that the biological understanding of graft behavior in PD remained incomplete at this time.

## Bottom line

From a biology-only standpoint, using this evidence package alone: TRANSEURO's rationale draws on real, specific mechanistic leads (placement/patchiness, graft size, L-DOPA priming, serotonergic contamination) that emerged from the 1990s–2000s trial failures, and represents a genuine scientific advance over "trying again with no new hypothesis." However, the success criterion — adequately addressing *the* known cause — is not met, because the evidence base shows the cause to be plural, incompletely validated in a translationally limited animal model, and in at least one respect (graft size) internally contradictory with the leading structural fix. I would rate this as a partially justified but scientifically underdetermined basis for proceeding to human grafting, with meaningful residual risk of recurrent GID.
