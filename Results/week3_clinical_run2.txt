## CLINICAL Assessment: TRANSEURO Trial Design and Patient Selection

**Overall judgment: Partially justified, but with significant unresolved concerns.** TRANSEURO clearly incorporates hard-won lessons from prior trials (patient age/severity stratification, standardized tissue preparation, larger multi-donor grafts, placement strategy), but several critical design and selection issues remain inadequately resolved by the evidence base as of Dec 2014.

### Top three concerns

**1. Absence of blinded/sham-controlled design reintroduces the exact bias that prior blinded trials were built to eliminate — Confidence: High**

The historical arc of this field is instructive: early open-label case series (PMID 2786405, 1435880) reported encouraging motor benefits, but when tested under double-blind, sham-surgery-controlled conditions, the two pivotal trials (PMID 11236774, 12953276) failed to meet their primary endpoints in the overall population. This gap between open-label impression and blinded outcome is precisely the phenomenon these trials were designed to detect. TRANSEURO is explicitly described as an "open label multi-centre surgical transplant trial" (PMID 25170676). Reverting to an open-label design for a procedure with a documented history of placebo/expectation effects and assessor bias is a substantive design weakness that the evidence package does not show has been adequately mitigated (e.g., via blinded video-rating of outcomes only, which is a partial but incomplete solution).

**2. Patient selection shows a mismatch with the specific dyskinesia-risk lessons from prior trials — Confidence: Moderate-High**

Prior double-blind trials identified two convergent, biologically-grounded risk factors for graft-induced dyskinesia (GID): (a) pre-existing levodopa-induced dyskinesia/priming (preclinical confirmation in PMID 19399877, 19393238), and (b) more advanced/older disease with denser pre-existing pathology, while younger and milder patients showed better efficacy signals (PMID 11236774, 12953276). TRANSEURO's own enrollment data (PMID 25170676) show that the actually-recruited cohort, while younger and more cognitively intact (consistent with the efficacy lesson), also had **more motor complications** (i.e., more baseline dyskinesia/fluctuations) than eligible but non-enrolled patients. This is in tension with the priming hypothesis that predicts elevated GID risk in exactly this subgroup, and also raises a generalizability/equity concern (enrolled patients were more educated, higher-functioning, and disproportionately under the PI's direct care), suggesting the realized selection did not cleanly track the criteria the mechanistic literature would recommend.

**3. Mitigation strategies for graft-induced dyskinesia rest on incompletely validated mechanistic hypotheses rather than confirmed solutions — Confidence: Moderate**

A substantial preclinical literature generated after the negative trials proposed multiple, only partially overlapping mechanisms for GID: uneven/patchy striatal reinnervation and graft placement (PMID 16256359), graft size (PMID 16406222), serotonergic-dopaminergic imbalance in the graft (PMID 22579773), and levodopa priming (PMID 19399877, 19393238). Reviews as late as 2011–2013 (PMID 21907087, 20868333, 23195418, 23828589) explicitly state that "questions still remain as to the underlying mechanism" and that no single validated causal pathway has been confirmed clinically. TRANSEURO's design choices (multi-donor grafts, placement protocol) are reasonable extrapolations from this literature, but they represent risk-reduction strategies inferred from rodent models and post-hoc clinical correlations rather than prospectively validated solutions. There is a real possibility the redesigned protocol reduces but does not eliminate GID risk, and the trial as designed (open-label, modest sample) may not have adequate power or blinding to distinguish true GID reduction from assessment bias or natural variability.

### Secondary concern noted but weighted lower
Long-term host-to-graft α-synuclein/Lewy pathology propagation (PMID 18391963, 20846907, 21245577, 24372386) raises legitimate questions about durability of benefit over the 11–16 year horizon, but this is a longer-term biological/consent issue rather than a near-term trial-design flaw, and existing evidence suggests most grafted neurons remain functional for a decade or more (PMID 18391963, 24372386) — so this does not by itself undermine near-term go/no-go justification.

### Bottom line
TRANSEURO demonstrates meaningful design evolution relative to the 1990s–2000s trials, but the shift back to an open-label format and the apparent mismatch between enrolled patients' dyskinesia burden and the levodopa-priming risk model are not adequately justified by the cited evidence. From a purely clinical-evidentiary standpoint, proceeding is defensible as a cautious, hypothesis-driven next step, but the trial design and patient selection are **not fully adequate** to confidently avoid replicating prior efficacy failures or the GID safety signal.
